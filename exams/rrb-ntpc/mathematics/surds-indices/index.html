    Surds & Indices for the RRB NTPC CBT

    Surds and indices are the algebra of powers and roots, and the RRB NTPC Mathematics paper uses them in two ways. Some questions ask for a direct value, such as the value of a power or a root, while others ask for a simplification, a rationalisation or the solution of an equation in which the unknown sits in the exponent. The whole chapter rests on a small set of laws, and once those are clear the questions are quick and formula-driven.

    A power is written as a base raised to an exponent. In two to the power five, the number two is the base and the five is the exponent or index, and the whole expression means two multiplied by itself five times, which is thirty-two. A root is the opposite idea: the square root of a number is the value that, multiplied by itself, gives the number. When a root cannot be taken exactly and the result is irrational, the expression is called a surd, so the square root of two is a surd while the square root of nine is not, because it equals three exactly.

    The laws of indices are the engine of the chapter. When two powers with the same base are multiplied, the exponents are added, so two to the three times two to the four is two to the seven, which is one hundred and twenty-eight. When they are divided, the exponents are subtracted, so five to the six divided by five to the four is five squared, which is twenty-five. When a power is itself raised to a power, the exponents are multiplied, so two cubed squared is two to the six, which is sixty-four. Any non-zero base raised to the power zero is one, and a negative exponent means the reciprocal, so two to the minus three is one over two cubed, which is one-eighth.

    Fractional exponents connect powers and roots. A power of one-half means the square root, so sixteen to the one-half is four. A power of one-third means the cube root, so twenty-seven to the one-third is three. A power such as two-thirds means the cube root raised to the square, so sixty-four to the two-thirds is four squared, which is sixteen. Reading a fractional exponent as a root followed by a power, or a power followed by a root, is the skill, and the order does not change the answer.

    A surd can often be simplified by pulling out a perfect square. The square root of eight is the square root of four times two, which is two times the square root of two. In the same way the square root of twelve is two root three, the square root of eighteen is three root two, the square root of thirty-two is four root two, the square root of fifty is five root two, and the square root of seventy-five is five root three. The rule is to split the number under the root into the largest perfect square factor and the rest, then take the square root of that factor out in front.

    Surds behave like algebraic terms when they are added or subtracted. Only like surds, that is surds with the same number under the root, can be combined, so two root three plus three root three is five root three, and five root two minus two root two is three root two. To add the square root of eighteen and the square root of eight, first simplify each to three root two and two root two, and then add to get five root two. Multiplication and division are easier, because the roots can be merged: the square root of two times the square root of eight is the square root of sixteen, which is four, and the square root of sixteen over twenty-five is four over five.

    Rationalising the denominator removes a root from the bottom of a fraction, which is the standard final form of an answer. To rationalise one over the square root of two, multiply the top and the bottom by the square root of two, giving the square root of two over two. When the denominator is a sum or a difference such as the square root of two plus one, multiply by its conjugate, which is the same expression with the sign of the second term changed. So one over the square root of two plus one becomes the square root of two minus one, because the denominator becomes two minus one, which is one.

    The conjugate is worth understanding well, because it is the key to a whole family of questions. The product of an expression and its conjugate uses the difference of two squares, so the square root of five plus the square root of two times the square root of five minus the square root of two is five minus two, which is three, a rational number. This is exactly why multiplying by the conjugate clears the root from the denominator, and the same idea answers questions such as one over the square root of seven plus the square root of five, which becomes the square root of seven minus the square root of five, all over two.

    Comparing surds is a question type of its own. Since the square root function increases with its argument, the larger number under the root gives the larger root, so the square root of five is greater than the square root of three. When the surds have coefficients, bring the coefficient inside the root before comparing: two root three is the square root of twelve and three root two is the square root of eighteen, so three root two is the greater. The same method shows that the square root of eight and two root two are equal, because eight is four times two.

    Equations with indices are solved by making the bases the same on both sides. If two to the power x equals thirty-two, write thirty-two as two to the fifth, so x is five. If three to the power x equals eighty-one, write eighty-one as three to the fourth, so x is four. If the unknown appears in the exponent with a coefficient, as in two to the power two x equals sixteen, write sixteen as two to the fourth, so two x is four and x is two. When the base is a fraction, the same method works, so one-half to the power x equals one-eighth gives x equal to three.

    Two more facts appear as direct questions. The value of any non-zero number divided by zero is undefined, and the square root of a negative number is not a real number, so such an option is wrong. The number of digits in a power of ten is the exponent plus one, so ten to the five has six digits. Knowing the powers of small numbers by heart, such as two to the ten equal to one thousand and twenty-four and three to the fourth equal to eighty-one, removes nearly all the work from the direct-value questions.

    The order of a surd is the index of the root. The square root has order two, the cube root has order three, and so on, so the cube root of eight has order three. The number under the root is called the radicand. Two surds are said to be like surds when they have the same order and the same radicand, and only like surds can be added or subtracted directly. Recognising the order and the radicand is the first step in almost every surd simplification.

    The best way to prepare this chapter is to memorise the laws of indices, the meaning of a fractional and a negative exponent, the perfect squares up to at least thirty and the perfect cubes up to at least fifteen, and the standard surd simplifications. Practise turning a mixed expression into a single power, and practise rationalising denominators with a sum or a difference. With these tools the surds and indices questions in the paper become reliable, formula-driven marks.

    Key facts to remember

    • a^m x a^n = a^(m+n); a^m / a^n = a^(m-n); (a^m)^n = a^(mn).
    • a^0 = 1 for any non-zero a; a^(-n) = 1/a^n.
    • a^(1/2) is the square root, a^(1/3) is the cube root, a^(m/n) is the n-th root raised to the m-th power.
    • A surd is an irrational root; √n is a surd only when n is not a perfect square.
    • To simplify √n, split off the largest perfect-square factor: √8 = 2√2, √50 = 5√2.
    • Only like surds (same radicand) can be added or subtracted directly.
    • √a x √b = √(ab); √a / √b = √(a/b).
    • To rationalise 1/√a, multiply by √a/√a; to rationalise 1/(√a+√b), multiply by the conjugate (√a-√b).
    • (√a+√b)(√a-√b) = a - b, a rational number.
    • Compare surds by squaring both sides, or by bringing the coefficient inside the root.
    • Solve a^x = b by writing both sides with the same base and equating the exponents.
    • Know the powers: 2^10 = 1024, 3^4 = 81, 5^3 = 125, and the cubes 1 to 15.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What exactly is a surd?

    A surd is an irrational root of a number that is not a perfect power. The square root of two is a surd because its value never ends or repeats, but the square root of nine is not a surd because it equals three exactly.

    How do I add two surds such as √18 and √8?

    First simplify each surd to its basic form, then add like surds. The square root of eighteen is three root two and the square root of eight is two root two, so the sum is five root two.

    Why do we multiply by the conjugate when rationalising?

    Because the product of a surd expression and its conjugate uses the difference of two squares and becomes rational. For example the square root of five plus the square root of two times the square root of five minus the square root of two is five minus two, which is three, so the root disappears from the denominator.

    How do I compare two surds quickly?

    Square both, or bring any coefficient inside the root and then compare the radicands. Since two root three is the square root of twelve and three root two is the square root of eighteen, three root two is the greater.

    What is the fastest way to solve an equation like 2^(2x) = 16?

    Write both sides with the same base and equate the exponents. Sixteen is two to the fourth, so two x equals four and x equals two. The method is the same whatever the base.

    এই অধ্যায়ৰ প্ৰস্তুতিৰ আটাইতকৈ ভাল উপায় হৈছে সূচকৰ নিয়ম, ভগ্ন আৰু ঋণাত্মক ঘাতাংকৰ অৰ্থ, অন্ততঃ ত্ৰিশলৈকে পূৰ্ণবৰ্গ আৰু অন্ততঃ পোন্ধৰলৈকে পূৰ্ণঘন, আৰু মানক কৰণী-সৰলীকৰণ মনত ৰখা। এটা মিশ্ৰ ৰাশিক এটা ঘাতলৈ সলনিওৱাৰ অভ্যাস কৰক, আৰু যোগফল বা পাৰ্থক্য থকা হৰ হৰমূলদ কৰাৰ অভ্যাস কৰক। এই সঁজুলিবোৰৰ সৈতে প্ৰশ্নপত্ৰৰ কৰণী আৰু সূচকৰ প্ৰশ্নবোৰ নিৰ্ভৰযোগ্য, সূত্ৰ-চালিত নম্বৰ হৈ পৰে।

    সূচকৰ নিয়মবোৰ প্ৰয়োগ কৰোঁতে এটা সাধাৰণ ভুল এৰি চলা উচিত। (a+b)^2 ক a^2 + b^2 বুলি লিখিব নোৱাৰি; ইয়াৰ সঠিক মান a^2 + 2ab + b^2। একেদৰে a^m + a^n ৰ সৈতে a^(m+n) সমান নহয়, কেৱল পূৰণতহে ঘাতাংক যোগ হয়। আৰু a^(−n) ক ঋণাত্মক সংখ্যা বুলি ভাবিব নালাগে; ই কেৱল প্ৰতিক্ৰম, গতিকে ২^−৩ ধনাত্মক ১/৮। এই সৰু বিভ্ৰান্তিবোৰেই পৰীক্ষাত ভুল উত্তৰৰ আটাইতকৈ সাধাৰণ কাৰণ, গতিকে প্ৰতিটো পদক্ষেপত নিয়মটো মনত পেলাই লিখাটো অভ্যাস কৰক।

    কৰণীৰ সৈতে কাম কৰোঁতে আন এটা উপযোগী কৌশল হৈছে কৰণীয়ক মৌলিক উৎপাদকত ভাঙি চোৱা। √৭২ বিচাৰিবলৈ ৭২ = ৩৬ × ২ বুলি লিখি √৩৬ × √২ = ৬√২ পোৱা যায়। একেইদৰে √৯৮ = √(৪৯×২) = ৭√২ আৰু √২৪৩ = √(৮১×৩) = ৯√৩। এটা কৰণী আটাইতকৈ সৰল ৰূপত আছে বুলি তেতিয়াহে ধৰা যায় যেতিয়া মূলৰ তলৰ সংখ্যাত একৰ বাহিৰে আন কোনো পূৰ্ণবৰ্গ উৎপাদক নাথাকে। এই পৰীক্ষাটো প্ৰতিবাৰ কৰাটো ভাল অভ্যাস, কাৰণ প্ৰশ্নই প্ৰায়ে উত্তৰ আটাইতকৈ সৰল ৰূপত বিচাৰে।

    মনত ৰখাৰ যোগ্য মূল তথ্য

    • a^m × a^n = a^(m+n); a^m / a^n = a^(m−n); (a^m)^n = a^(mn)।
    • a^0 = ১ যিকোনো অশূন্য a ৰ বাবে; a^(−n) = ১/a^n।
    • a^(১/২) বৰ্গমূল, a^(১/৩) ঘনমূল, a^(m/n) হৈছে n-সংখ্যক মূলক m-সংখ্যক ঘাতলৈ তোলা।
    • কৰণী এটা অপৰিমেয় মূল; √n কৰণী হয় কেৱল যেতিয়া n পূৰ্ণবৰ্গ নহয়।
    • √n সৰল কৰিবলৈ আটাইতকৈ ডাঙৰ পূৰ্ণবৰ্গ উৎপাদক পৃথক কৰক: √৮ = ২√২, √৫০ = ৫√২।
    • কেৱল সদৃশ কৰণী (একে কৰণীয়) পোনপটীয়াকৈ যোগ বা বিয়োগ কৰিব পাৰি।
    • √a × √b = √(ab); √a / √b = √(a/b)।
    • ১/√a হৰমূলদ কৰিবলৈ √a/√a ৰে পূৰণ কৰক; ১/(√a+√b) হৰমূলদ কৰিবলৈ সংযুগ্ম (√a−√b) ৰে পূৰণ কৰক।
    • (√a+√b)(√a−√b) = a − b, এটা পৰিমেয় সংখ্যা।
    • দুয়ো ফাল বৰ্গ কৰি, নাইবা গুণাংক মূলৰ ভিতৰলৈ আনি কৰণী তুলনা কৰক।
    • a^x = b সমাধান কৰিবলৈ দুয়ো ফাল একে ভূমিৰে লিখি ঘাতাংকবোৰ সমান কৰক।
    • ঘাতবোৰ জানক: ২^১০ = ১০২৪, ৩^৪ = ৮১, ৫^৩ = ১২৫, আৰু ১ ৰ পৰা ১৫ লৈ ঘন।

    সততে সোধা প্ৰশ্ন

    কৰণী ঠিক কি?

    কৰণী হৈছে এনে এটা সংখ্যাৰ অপৰিমেয় মূল যি এটা পূৰ্ণ ঘাত নহয়। ২ ৰ বৰ্গমূল এটা কৰণী কাৰণ ইয়াৰ মান কেতিয়াও শেষ বা পুনৰাবৃত্তি নহয়, কিন্তু ৯ ৰ বৰ্গমূল কৰণী নহয় কাৰণ ই ঠিক তিনিৰ সমান।

    √১৮ আৰু √৮ ৰ দৰে দুটা কৰণী কেনেকৈ যোগ কৰিম?

    প্ৰথমে প্ৰতিটো কৰণীক ইয়াৰ মূল ৰূপলৈ সৰল কৰক, তাৰ পিছত সদৃশ কৰণী যোগ কৰক। √১৮ = ৩√২ আৰু √৮ = ২√২, গতিকে যোগফল ৫√২।

    হৰমূলদ কৰোঁতে সংযুগ্মৰে কিয় পূৰণ কৰোঁ?

    কাৰণ এটা কৰণী ৰাশি আৰু ইয়াৰ সংযুগ্মৰ গুণফলে দুটা বৰ্গৰ পাৰ্থক্য ব্যৱহাৰ কৰি পৰিমেয় হৈ পৰে। উদাহৰণস্বৰূপে (√৫+√২)(√৫−√২) = ৫−২ = ৩, গতিকে হৰৰ পৰা মূল আঁতৰি যায়।

    দুটা কৰণী দ্ৰুতভাৱে কেনেকৈ তুলনা কৰিম?

    দুয়োটাকে বৰ্গ কৰক, নাইবা যিকোনো গুণাংক মূলৰ ভিতৰলৈ আনি কৰণীয়বোৰ তুলনা কৰক। যিহেতু ২√৩ = √১২ আৰু ৩√২ = √১৮, ৩√২ ডাঙৰ।

    ২^(২x) = ১৬ ৰ দৰে সমীকৰণ সমাধান কৰাৰ আটাইতকৈ দ্ৰুত উপায় কি?

    দুয়ো ফাল একে ভূমিৰে লিখি ঘাতাংকবোৰ সমান কৰক। ষোল্ল হৈছে ২^৪, গতিকে ২x = ৪ আৰু x = ২। ভূমি যিয়েই নহওক পদ্ধতি একেই।
